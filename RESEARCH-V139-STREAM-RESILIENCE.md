# RESEARCH-V139 — STREAM RESILIENCE (THE UNBROKEN STREAM research)

**For:** the doomalay v1.40.0 wave ("THE UNBROKEN STREAM") · **Agent:** web-research stream · **Repo state reconed:** v1.38.0 tip (read-only)
**Symptoms under study:** (1) backgrounding the Android app pauses all bots; on return the panel log shows no work done in absence. (2) Losing internet pauses/kills the bots instead of the work continuing/resuming with full catch-up.

## Thesis (one paragraph)

Doomalay's architecture is already 80% of the way to the unbreakable stream: the turn runs **server-side** in the Go engine, survives client socket death (v1.35 THE UNBROKEN TURN — `chatPipe` swap, `&since=N` seq replay, no fabricated terminals), and the WS transport already separates *connection* liveness from *run* liveness. The two user-visible failures are not protocol failures — they are **CPU starvation** and **retry-policy starvation**. When the screen turns off, Android suspends the CPU and no foreground service holds a wake lock by itself; doomalay's `EngineService` holds none (no `WAKE_LOCK` permission in the manifest, no `PowerManager` call in the Kotlin), so the engine process literally stops being scheduled and the bots "pause." When the internet dies, the engine's `netPauseLadder` retries for only ~110 seconds total (1.5s/3s/15s/30s/60s rungs) and refuses to retry any round that already streamed visible content — a real outage (>2 min, or mid-stream) kills the turn terminally. The fixes are documented, boring, and precedented: **partial wake lock + foreground service (the Termux law), battery-optimization exemption for deep Doze, an unbounded connectivity-gated retry loop woken by `ConnectivityManager.NetworkCallback` (no fixed timers), jittered exponential backoff, and a resume-with-partial-content rule for mid-stream failures** — all riding the seq-based event log that already exists for catch-up. Android's own laws (app freezing, Doze, the Android 15 `dataSync` 6-hour timeout, FGS type enforcement) all have cited escape hatches that fit doomalay's exact shape.

## §1 ANDROID BACKGROUND LAWS (symptom 1's home turf)

### 1.1 — The cached-app freezer: zero CPU, in RAM
> "Android 11 (API level 30) or higher supports the cached apps freezer. This feature stops execution for cached processes… The cached apps freezer keeps apps in RAM while keeping them off the CPU… Android freezes cached apps by migrating their processes into a frozen cgroup." — and on Android 14+: "App processes in the cached state are frozen **10 seconds** after entering the cached state." "When an app process is frozen, all of its threads are suspended and can't perform CPU work until unfrozen."
> — https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/cached-apps-freezer
> "When an app is frozen, it receives zero CPU time and can't do any work." — https://source.android.com/docs/core/architecture/ipc/binder-freezer

**Consequences for doomalay:** a backgrounded app *with no active foreground service* becomes "cached" (https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/process-lifecycle: "Since cached processes can be killed by the system at any time, apps should cease all work while in the cached state") and is frozen within ~10s on Android 14+ — JS, sockets, everything. doomalay's app process hosts `EngineService` (a `dataSync` FGS), which keeps the app's process out of the cached state (the freezer doc itself distinguishes "apps that stay in a lifecycle without a UI, such as apps with a foreground service" as non-frozen; the process-lifecycle doc ranks FGS-hosting processes above cached). **So the WebView's JS and the engine's parent process are *not* frozen while `EngineService` runs.** The engine is a child process spawned via `ProcessBuilder` — cgroup membership is inherited from the parent process at fork, so it rides the app process's scheduling fate, but AMS does not track it as an FGS host on its own: its continued scheduling is an implementation detail, not a documented guarantee. The *documented* guarantee is the wake lock (§1.3).

### 1.2 — CPU sleep is not Doze, and foreground services do NOT hold a wake lock for you
> "In Doze mode, the system attempts to conserve battery by restricting apps' access to network and CPU-intensive services… The system applies the following restrictions… **Suspends network access. Ignores wake locks.**"
> — https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby

Two separate mechanisms kill background CPU on Android:
1. **Device suspend (CPU sleep).** Seconds after the screen goes off, the kernel suspends unless something holds a wake lock. A foreground service keeps the *process* alive and above the cached/freezer line — it does **not** keep the *CPU* awake. The official best practice:
   > "In practice, this means you should get and hold the wakelock in a **foreground service**. Foreground services are required to display a notification." — https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/awake/wakelock/best-practices (also: keep acquire/release logic simple; always release; use a stable hard-coded tag)
   > "Partial wake locks are a mechanism in the PowerManager API that let developers keep the CPU running after a device's display turns off." — https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/issues/stuck-wakelock (and /excessive-wakelock)
2. **Doze (light + deep).** Screen-off, on-battery idle throttling. In *deep* Doze (device stationary + unplugged + screen off), even wake locks are ignored and network is suspended — except for exempted apps:
   > "An app that is partially exempt **can use the network and hold partial wake locks during Doze and App Standby**… An app can check whether it is currently on the exemption list by calling `isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()`." — https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby (Acceptable use cases for exemption)

**This is the Termux law** (the known-good reference for running a local server on Android):
> "termux-wake-lock and termux-wake-unlock can be used to trigger a wakelock which causes Android not to go into deep sleep." — https://wiki.termux.com/wiki/Termux-wake-lock — Termux pairs this with its own foreground-service notification ("Acquire wakelock" in the notification), i.e. **FGS + PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK together**.

**App Standby Buckets** (screen-off but device moving — the pocket case): "Regular jobs, expedited jobs, alarms and network access can be limited based on the app standby bucket." — https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details ; buckets: https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby . An app hosting an active FGS sits in the active/working range, so bucket restrictions are not the primary risk for doomalay; Doze + suspend are.

### 1.3 — WebView in a backgrounded-but-foreground-service'd app
- `WebView.onPause()`: "Does a best-effort attempt to pause any processing that can be paused safely, such as animations and geolocation. **Note that this call does not pause JavaScript.**" — https://developer.android.com/reference/android/webkit/WebView (method `onPause()`). `pauseTimers()`/`resumeTimers()` (which *do* pause JS timers app-wide) are **manual** — the platform does not call them for you. doomalay's `MainActivity.onPause()` only forwards to `panelSheet` (the in-app browser sheet); the main WebView's JS is left running.
- Chromium still throttles *hidden* pages: "Intensive throttling takes effect when the page has been hidden for more than 5 minutes, the page has been silent for at least 30 seconds…" (down to 1 timer wake-up per minute) — https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88 ; and on Android, "Faster background freezing on Android — Shortens the time to freezing background pages (and associated workers) from 5 minutes to 1 minute on Android." — https://chromestatus.com/feature/5386725031149568 . MDN: "Timers such as setTimeout() are throttled in background/inactive tabs." — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API
- **Net law for doomalay:** treat the PWA in the WebView as a *view*, never the *carrier* of continuity. JS timers in the backgrounded WebView may be throttled to ~1/min, but the WS connection itself (kernel-level) and the engine-side turn are unaffected — and on `visibilitychange → visible` the PWA must resync from the transcript, not trust in-memory state. doomalay's PWA already has the visible-catch-up pass (chatpanel.js `visibilitychange` listener) and a 20s background revive poll; the revive poll *will* be throttled by the Chrome-88-style laws — that's fine **because** the engine owns the turn and `&since=` replay owns the truth.
- Web sockets in a frozen process die silently; in an unfrozen process they stay open (network-stack-level, no JS needed). The engine's 20s-ping/75s-read-deadline (chat.go `wsPingEvery`/`wsReadDeadline`, "Half-dead sockets (tunnels, doze) used to linger forever") is the correct half-open detector — and crucially a fired read deadline only clears the *pipe*, never the *turn* (v1.35 law).

### 1.4 — Foreground service types: current enforcement timeline
- **Android 14 (API 34, doomalay's current target):** every FGS must declare a type; missing/mismatched type = `MissingForegroundServiceTypeException` / `InvalidForegroundServiceTypeException`. — https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/fgs-types-required and https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types . doomalay already declares `dataSync` correctly (manifest + `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC` + `startForeground(..., FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_DATA_SYNC)`).
- **Android 15 (API 35) — THE TIMEOUT TRAP:**
  > "If an app targets Android 15 or higher… The system permits dataSync and mediaProcessing foreground services to run for a total of **6 hours in a 24-hour period**, after which the system calls the running service's `Service.onTimeout(int, int)`… When the system calls `Service.onTimeout()`, the service is no longer considered a foreground service. If the service does not call `Service.stopSelf()`, the system throws an internal exception… if the user brings the app to the foreground, the timer resets."
  > — https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout (behavior change: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)
  
  doomalay targets SDK 34 today, so this is *not yet live* for its builds — it becomes live the day targetSdk is bumped to 35. An always-on local engine outruns 6h/24h easily.
- **The escape hatch — `specialUse`:** type "Covers any valid foreground service use cases that aren't covered by the other foreground service types"; manifest permission `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE`; constant `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_SPECIAL_USE`; **Runtime prerequisites: None.** The free-form `<property>` use-case declaration is only reviewed "when you submit your app in the Google Play Console" — irrelevant for doomalay's F-Droid/CI distribution. — https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types#special-use
- **Android 12+:** starting an FGS *from the background* is restricted — https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start . doomalay starts `EngineService` from `MainActivity` (user interaction) → unaffected; `START_STICKY` + the existing watchdog already cover engine-death recovery.

## §2 THE CATCH-UP PROTOCOL LAWS (the client-side contract)

### 2.1 — SSE gives you the reconnect handshake, nothing else
> "The `Last-Event-ID` HTTP request header reports an EventSource object's last event ID string to the server when the user agent is to reestablish the connection." — https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/server-sent-events.html (§9.2 Server-sent events)
> MDN "Using server-sent events": the server sends `id:`; the browser's EventSource reconnects automatically and replays the last event ID. — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Server-sent_events/Using_server-sent_events

### 2.2 — The robust pattern: cursor/offset event log ("send me everything after seq N") + replay buffer + live tail
The industry consensus for LLM streaming reconnection is exactly the shape doomalay already built:
> "Server-Sent Events ships a Last-Event-ID header. When an SSE connection drops, the browser sends it automatically on reconnect, and the server can pick up from there… **SSE has no native history beyond what you build server-side.**" — Ably, "Resume tokens and last-event IDs for LLM streaming" (storage design, deduplication, gap detection, distributed session state): https://ably.com/blog/resume-tokens-last-event-id-llm-streaming-reconnection
> **resumable-stream** (the package behind Vercel AI SDK's resumable streams): "Library for wrapping streams of strings… in a way that a client can resume them after they lost a connection, **or to allow a second client to follow along**. Designed for use in serverless environments without sticky load balancing. The library relies on a pubsub mechanism… In that common case the library performs a single INCR and SUBSCRIBE per stream." — https://www.npmjs.com/package/resumable-stream (usage: `resumableStream(streamId, makeStream, resumeAt)`; resume from an explicit integer offset; server keeps writing the stream into durable buffer while clients come and go)
> Vercel AI SDK "Chatbot Resume Streams": "client-side aborts are treated as disconnects" (the generation continues server-side; a returning client resumes at its offset). — https://ai-sdk.dev/docs/ai-sdk-ui/chatbot-resume-streams

**The named pattern:** single writer (the run/turn) → append-only, seq-numbered event log → per-subscriber cursors → replay-from-cursor + live tail fan-out → client-side dedup by seq. Idempotent, because the log is the truth and every replay converges. doomalay's `chat_events` table with per-event `seq`, `&since=N` incremental replay + live feed, client dedup by `ev.i`, and `replay_done` gating (chat.go `handleChatWS`) **is this pattern, already shipped.** The wave's job is to extend it, not invent it.

### 2.3 — How the big chat apps behave (backgrounded/offline)
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI, official):** the Voice mode feature flag is the explicit product statement: "You can continue a Voice conversation while using other apps or while your phone is locked. Background conversations can be controlled under Settings → Voice." — https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice . That is: **FGG/foreground-service-class machinery + notifications during long generations, generation continuing server-side.** For text turns, even OpenAI's apps have reported backgrounded-turn interruption (community evidence, not a spec): https://community.openai.com/t/automatically-hand-off-long-running-chat-turns-to-work-when-the-app-is-backgrounded/1402176 — the server-side transcript is the resync source on return, which is why a lost foreground turn is recoverable: the transcript refetch catches up.
- **The engineering receipt for that behavior** is the resumable-stream/AI-SDK family above (OpenAI's own library lineage) — server-side continuation + offset resync. Claude's web app shows the same contract (server continues the turn; reopening refetches the transcript; observed behavior — no public engineering post to cite).
- **The law to copy:** generation NEVER lives in the client. The client is a tailer of the log. On reconnect/reopen: replay from cursor, then live.

### 2.4 — Page Visibility API: resync on visible, never trust memory
> "The Page Visibility API provides events you can watch for to know when a document becomes visible or hidden" — and the docs pair it with the timer-throttling warning, i.e. the sanctioned pattern is: on `visibilitychange → visible`, refresh state from the source of truth. — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API

doomalay's PWA already runs a one-pass catch-up render on visible (chatpanel.js) and the v1.35 truth-arm re-arms streaming from the transcript's truth after `replay_done`. **Gap:** the resync currently rides the WS reconnect ladder (up to 20s revive poll + 75s server read deadline). On Android, `MainActivity.onResume` can *force* the event immediately (it already owns the `document.dispatchEvent(new Event('visibilitychange'))` JS bridge for auth returns — the same tool applies to bot resync).

## §3 GO ENGINE LAWS (the server internals)

### 3.1 — `Request.Context()` dies with the client: the classic detached-work law
> "For incoming server requests, the context is canceled when the client's connection closes, the request is canceled (with HTTP/2), or when the ServeHTTP method returns." — https://pkg.go.dev/net/http#Request.Context (quoted and proven live in https://brandur.org/fragments/testing-request-cancellation ; see also the net/http tracking issue https://github.com/golang/go/issues/70834 )
> The cure (Go 1.21+): "WithoutCancel returns a derived context that points to the parent context and **is not canceled when parent is canceled**. The returned context returns no Deadline or Err, and its Done channel is nil." — https://pkg.go.dev/context#WithoutCancel

The law: long work owned by a *run manager* (session/turn registry, its own `context.WithCancel(context.Background())` + explicit Stop) — never by the HTTP/WS request. doomalay is already on the right side: chat.go line ~1335 `turnCtx, turnCancel := context.WithCancel(context.Background())` + `registerTurnCancel(sessionID, turnCancel, sender)`; the WS handler's ctx only owns the pipe/ping loop. The wave must only keep this law when adding new long paths (e.g. the net-regained waiter loop must NOT be parented on any request ctx).

### 3.2 — Per-subscriber fan-out: drop-on-slow, buffered channels, no shared request ctx
The pattern used across the Go ecosystem (and by doomalay's own `chatPipe` + cap-32 progress channels in hub.go): each subscriber gets its own buffered send channel; a full/slow subscriber is dropped (connection closed or skipped) and later healed by *replay from the log* — the writer never blocks on the slowest reader. This pairs with the event-log law of §2.2: the log is the durable path; the live fan-out is a best-effort fast path. (Replay-based healing on slow/dropped consumers is the same contract as resumable-stream's "second client follows along" and Ably's gap detection.)

### 3.3 — `http.ResponseController` / write deadlines for streaming
> "NewResponseController returns a ResponseController for a request… allows per-request ReadDeadline/WriteDeadline" — Go 1.20's fix for the "global WriteTimeout kills SSE" trap (you can clear a server-wide deadline per request: "You can effectively remove the server-wide deadline on a per-request basis by passing a zero-valued time.Time to SetWriteDeadline()"). — https://pkg.go.dev/net/http#NewResponseController , design issue https://github.com/golang/go/issues/54136 , practical guide https://www.alexedwards.net/blog/how-to-use-the-http-responsecontroller-type

doomalay currently sets `ReadTimeout: 0, WriteTimeout: 0` globally (server.go ~588) with an `IdleTimeout` — a legal, simple posture for a stream-heavy local server (half-open WS is instead handled by the 20s ping / 75s read deadline pair, which is stronger than any write deadline for detecting a gone client). The ResponseController law is the alternative if the server ever needs a global write timeout for non-streaming routes.

### 3.4 — WebSocket keepalive vs mobile backgrounding: silence ≠ dead run
> gorilla/websocket: "SetPingHandler sets the handler for ping messages received from the peer. The default ping handler sends a pong to the peer." — https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/gorilla/websocket (control frames: ping/pong are protocol-level, answered by the *network stack*, not by page JS — so a JS-throttled background WebView still pongs; a *frozen* or *suspended* process does not.)

**The law:** ping/pong measures *connection* liveness only. A silent client (frozen, suspended, dozing, offline WebView) must never be interpreted as a dead *run*. doomalay's split is already correct: read-deadline → `pipe.clear()` (connection gone) while `turnCtx` (run) lives on with its own pulse (`turnActivity`/`markTurnEvent`, v1.35). The one Go-side hazard to keep in the wave's spec: the ping loop and all turn work must stay on Background-derived contexts (they are), and nothing in the new code paths may accidentally select on the WS handler's ctx.

### 3.5 — Outbound LLM retries: backoff + jitter + interruptible waits
> "Exponential Backoff And Jitter" (the canonical study): backoff alone synchronizes thundering herds; **full jitter** (`sleep = random_between(0, min(cap, base * 2**attempt))`) is the best trade; equal/decorrelated jitter are the alternatives. — https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/exponential-backoff-and-jitter (author's companion post: https://brooker.co.za/blog/2015/03/21/backoff.html ; "adding exponential backoff alone is not enough — the key insight is that randomizing each client's retry…")
> Retry waits must be **interruptible** (select on ctx.Done + a wake signal), never blind `time.Sleep` — the Go context law of §3.1 applied to waiting.

### 3.6 — Mid-flight stream failures: retrying the remainder is a *server-side log* problem, not a provider problem
Providers' completion streams are not byte-offset resumable (no `Last-Event-ID` on the provider leg); the resumable-stream/AI-SDK lineage (§2.2) solves the *server→client* leg by keeping the server's own buffer and letting the client resume at an offset. The accepted patterns for the *engine→provider* leg: (a) retry the round **only if nothing visible was emitted** (clean restart, idempotent), (b) if partial content was emitted: persist the partial as the honest record and continue the conversation (the partial becomes context for the next call — the ReAct loop already speaks this shape), or (c) pause the turn (ladder) rather than terminate. doomalay's current rule — "rounds that already streamed VISIBLE content are never retried (double-render)" (chat.go `netPauseLadder` comment) — is the conservative (a)-only form; the wave's choice space is (b)/(c) above with the event log absorbing the seam.

## §4 NETWORK-LOSS LAWS (symptom 2)

### 4.1 — A local engine with no internet can do exactly one thing: keep the work, retry when the network returns
When the device loses all connectivity, a loopback server (127.0.0.1) and the local PWA↔engine leg stay fully functional — only the engine's *outbound* provider calls fail. There is no Android API that conjures bandwidth; the accepted pattern is **queue/pause + retry loop + idempotent event log** (AWS backoff+jitter law §3.5; Ably "gap detection" §2.2). What Android gives you is the *fast, event-driven wake*:

### 4.2 — Detect regain with `ConnectivityManager.NetworkCallback`, not fixed timers
> "Apps can register a callback with ConnectivityManager to be alerted to changes that the app cares about. Using the callback, your app can **react immediately** to [connectivity changes]." — https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/network-ops/reading-network-state (registerNetworkCallback / `onAvailable(Network)` / `onLost` / NET_CAPABILITY_VALIDATED; API-24+ = doomalay's minSdk)
> Doze note: the callback fires and unfreezes lifecycle events — "The system immediately unfreezes a frozen app process during a lifecycle event. These events include receiving an intent, starting a job service…" — https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/cached-apps-freezer

**The law:** fixed-interval retry timers are the *backstop*, never the trigger. The Kotlin layer (which owns the platform APIs) listens for validated connectivity and pushes the event into the engine over loopback (the exact shape of the existing v1.17.2 `TermuxBridgeServer` 127.0.0.1 token-authed bridge — or a direct engine route); the engine broadcasts "net regained" to every waiting retry waiter (§3.5 interruptible waits). This kills both the "waited 60s after the network was already back" waste and the "ladder expired during a longer outage" kill.

### 4.3 — Deep Doze suspends network even for wakelock holders (the exemption is the only escape)
See §1.2: in deep Doze, network access is suspended and wake locks are ignored — "An app that is partially exempt can use the network and hold partial wake locks during Doze and App Standby" (battery-optimization exemption via `isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()` / `ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`). For a device sitting still on battery with the screen off for a long time, this — not the wakelock — is what keeps the LLM calls alive. doomalay's Termux-style peers all surface this ask (Termux itself instructs users to disable battery optimization for servers).

### 4.4 — What the local engine must NOT do when offline
- Don't kill the turn on the first `context deadline exceeded` / `connection refused` — the `isTransientNetErr` classifier (chat.go ~2000) already recognizes the whole transient family (timeout, reset, broken pipe, unexpected EOF, refused, tls bad record mac…). Terminal errors (auth/4xx) stay terminal.
- Don't let the turn's own `ctx` deadline fire while paused-on-network (pause state ≠ active work; the turn budget must treat a network-paused round as suspended time, or the budget kills the turn while it's doing exactly what the user asked: waiting).
- Don't fabricate a completion (the v1.35 honesty law) — the pause marker is ephemeral (live-only), the terminal is the engine's truth.

| **6** | **RESUME-ON-FOCUS FORCED RESYNC (PWA + MainActivity).** On `visibilitychange → visible` (and on `MainActivity.onResume`, which already owns the synthetic-visibilitychange JS bridge), if a turn is live: force an immediate liveness probe of the WS (client `ping` or a tiny `/api/health` fetch) — if not answered in ~1s, tear down and reconnect **now** instead of waiting for the server's 75s read deadline or the 20s revive poll; then replay `&since=lastSeq`. Never trust in-memory streaming state past a hidden period. | §2.4 (Page Visibility best practice), §1.3 (half-open sockets after doze) | **Symptom 1's** "on return the panel doesn't show the work": with #1 the engine worked; this makes the panel prove it within ~1s of return instead of up to 75s. |
| **7** | **SPECIAL-USE FGS (forward-looking, targetSdk 35).** When the target bumps to 35, either (a) move `EngineService` to `foregroundServiceType="specialUse"` + `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE` + `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_SPECIAL_USE` (runtime prerequisites: **None**; the Play-review `<property>` form doesn't apply to F-Droid/CI distribution), or (b) stay `dataSync` and implement `Service.onTimeout(int,int)` → graceful engine drain + `stopSelf()` + restart-from-foreground. (a) is the honest fit for an always-on local engine; the 6h/24h dataSync budget cannot hold a server. | §1.4 (fgs/timeout + service-types#special-use) | **Symptom 1's future regression:** Android 15's 6-hour dataSync cap would otherwise kill the engine mid-wave (RemoteServiceException). |
| **8** | **THE LIVE NOTIFICATION (cheap, rides #1).** While any bot turn is live, update the FGS notification (low-importance channel, already exists) with "N bots working · last event hh:mm"; on all-idle, revert to "Engine running." Pure `NotificationManager.notify` on the existing channel — the user-visible receipt that work continues in absence. | §1.2 (the user should be aware — the notification IS the honesty) | **Symptom 1's** trust problem: the returning user sees the engine was alive before they even open a panel. |

**Order of attack:** #1 and #3 are the two load-bearing fixes (CPU + retry policy — they directly answer the two complaints). #4 unlocks #3's speed. #6 + #8 make the catch-up *visible*. #2 covers the deep-doze corner. #5 is the deepest engine change (touches the double-render invariant — needs its own rig law: "a mid-stream-cut round resumes with a persisted marker, never a fabricated clean end"). #7 is a two-line future guard.

**The wave's invariants (carry them forward):**
- The PWA never owns continuity; the `chat_events` seq log owns truth; every new engine state (net-paused, net-regained, round-continued) rides the same log or the same live-only ephemerality rules as v1.35's.
- Connection liveness ≠ run liveness (pipe clear on read-deadline; turn ctx on `context.Background()`; the new waiter loops inherit the same detachment).
- No fabricated terminals — pauses and continuations are *marked*, endings come from the engine's truth.
- Battery honesty: wakelock only while the service (and its notification) lives; drop the wakelock when all bots are idle (an idle engine doesn't need the CPU awake — a "wakelock while any turn is live" refinement, documented as the stretch goal).

**PWA/WebView service-worker verdict (§ topic 5):** raw ServiceWorker *registration* is reported supported in Android WebView since version 40 (caniwebview/BCD), but **background/periodic work is not**: PeriodicSyncManager is "n" for androidwebview (caniwebview data file), and Background Sync's browser-side scheduler does not exist in a WebView host. A service worker in doomalay's WebView can only cache the app shell (which sw.js already does, and deliberately never intercepts `/api/*`). **SWs are a dead end for the unbroken stream — the engine is the continuity layer, exactly as architected.**
